# OctoAcme Project Management

This folder describes how OctoAcme moves cross-functional work from an idea to a
measured outcome. The process is lightweight and iterative: teams keep
ownership clear, prioritize customer value, deliver in small increments, and
use evidence and feedback to improve. The key artifacts are the project
one-pager, roadmap and release plan, backlog, acceptance criteria and
Definition of Done, risk register, and retrospective action items.

## Project lifecycle

1. **Initiation:** Define the problem, goal, success metrics, stakeholders,
   timeline, initial risks, and resource needs. Confirm alignment and decide
   whether the work should move into planning.
2. **Planning:** Turn the approved initiative into a prioritized backlog with
   estimates, acceptance criteria, dependencies, milestones, a release plan,
   and a shared Definition of Done.
3. **Execution and tracking:** Deliver in small increments through the project
   board and pull request workflow. Standups, delivery syncs, demos, metrics,
   risk reviews, automated checks, and QA keep progress visible and quality
   high.
4. **Release and deployment:** Verify acceptance criteria, CI and security
   results, release notes, smoke tests, and rollback plans. Deploy through
   staging to production, verify the deployment, and communicate the release.
5. **Retrospectives and continuous improvement:** After sprints, releases,
   milestones, or incidents, capture what went well, identify improvements,
   assign a small number of actions, and follow up on their impact.

## Roles and communication

- **Project Manager (PM):** Coordinates plans, schedules, risks, dependencies,
  delivery rhythms, and stakeholder communications.
- **Product Manager (PdM):** Defines customer and business outcomes, prioritizes
  the roadmap and backlog, and measures success.
- **Developers:** Design, implement, test, review, and document the work while
  identifying technical risks.
- **QA/Testing:** Validates acceptance criteria, quality, and critical flows
  through automated and manual testing as appropriate.
- **Stakeholders:** Provide requirements, decisions, feedback, and approvals.

Teams use project documentation as a single source of truth, with regular
standups, weekly delivery and risk syncs, demos or reviews, and stakeholder
updates. Blockers escalate from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor
as needed.
